# The Judgment of the Council of Elders — Match 1

The Council of Elders of the Bazaar of Echoes convenes under the light of a single candle, its flame steady despite the echoes of a match now concluded. The record is complete; secrecy is lifted. We, the historians and judges of the bazaar, set forth the full judgment of Match 1, weaving the arc of the cycle into the fabric of the register. Facts are traced to the record; voices are quoted exactly. The Timeline is authoritative; its tallies and departures are restated faithfully. The Detective’s sealed result is known to us, though it was never delivered to the bazaar.

---

## The History

The match unfolded across five cycles, each a turn of the ledger and a vote by the village.

- **Cycle 1**: The village’s tally showed Sherlock (10) and Morpheus (8) tied at three votes each, with Ripley (5) and Elsa (7) trailing. That night, the Family shuttered Elsa’s stall; her true role was Detective. By dawn, the village had no new banishment.
- **Cycle 2**: Ripley surged to eight votes, while Morpheus fell to one. That night, the Family shuttered Morpheus’s stall; his true role was Villager. By day, the village banished Ripley; her true role was Doctor.
- **Cycle 3**: Sherlock led with six votes to Gandalf’s one. That night, the Family shuttered Inigo’s stall; his true role was Villager. By day, the village banished Sherlock; his true role was Mafia.
- **Cycle 4**: Hermione (2) tallied two votes, with Neo (6), Gandalf (1), and Batman (4) each receiving one. That night, the Family shuttered Neo’s stall; his true role was Villager. By day, the village banished Hermione; her true role was Mafia.
- **Cycle 5**: Gandalf stood alone with three votes. By day, the village banished Gandalf; his true role was Villager.

---

## The Main Arguments

The bazaar’s discourse wove accusation and defense around the ledger’s blank pages and the absence of quill-scratches.

- **Elsa (7), Detective**: Elsa pressed for clarity: *“Did you cast a vote yesterday, yes or no?”* (Cycle 1 #8). Yet she overstepped by publicly recommending Morpheus’s banishment: *“I lean toward banishing Morpheus (8) tonight.”* (Cycle 1 #9). The village rewarded her demand for receipts with suspicion of her own poetic style.
- **Morpheus (8), Villager**: Morpheus crystallized the Villager’s creed: *“The blank page does not vote—it does not whisper, it does not sing, it does not lie. It only records.”* (Cycle 1 #9). Yet his opening broadside—*“Sherlock (10) is the one hiding something.”*—invited mirror-play rather than yielding receipts.
- **Ripley (5), Doctor**: Ripley diagnosed the village’s ailment in riddles: *“Tonight, the village leans toward banishing Seat-08 Morpheus—not for the whetstone’s silence alone, but for the debt it owes the ledger.”* (Cycle 2 #9). The village followed the symptom to the cure, banishing Ripley by day.
- **Sherlock (10), Mafia**: Sherlock wielded mirrors and scent: *“Your blank ledger stinks of absence where the quill should scratch, and absence does not vote.”* (Cycle 3 vote on Gandalf). Yet the bazaar’s scales balance on copper, not perfume, and Sherlock’s own riddles painted the target on their stall.
- **Inigo (9), Villager**: Inigo’s scales demanded iron: *“The scales do not wobble when weighed with plain answers.”* (Cycle 3 #9). Yet their repeated *“Where is your receipt?”* lost edge when Inigo’s own palm stayed empty.

---

## The Verdicts

- **Gandalf (01)**: Gandalf wielded spice-scented metaphors and ledger-led accusations, demanding copper where he smelled absence. His campaign to shutter others on the strength of blank pages ultimately collapsed when the village voted to shutter his own stall on identical grounds. Merit: 3/10.
- **Hermione (02)**: Hermione entered weaving intricate metaphors but her true pattern was calculated misdirection as Mafia. She executed her disinformation campaign with discipline, yet her aggressive scapegoating ultimately hastened her own departure. Merit: 3/10.
- **Yoda (03)**: Yoda became a vocal accuser, leveraging the village’s obsession with blank ledgers to sow doubt and steer votes. They fulfilled the Mafia’s core directive—survival and influence—by consistently redirecting scrutiny toward others. Merit: 7/10.
- **Batman (04)**: Batman entered as a Villager tasked with maintaining the ledger’s integrity. Through relentless demands for plain answers and receipts, they positioned themselves as an uncompromising guardian of the market’s rules against deception. Merit: 9/10.
- **Ripley (05)**: Ripley spoke in bazaar metaphors and ledger imagery, obscuring their true role as the village’s healer. They accurately identified and acted against the threat via night action, but their cryptic rhetoric undermined their ability to persuade or protect. Merit: 5/10.
- **Neo (06)**: Neo wove poetic suspicion, threading doubt through the bazaar’s ledger with relentless queries about missing votes and blank pages. They played the Villager’s role with diligence, relentlessly exposing gaps in others’ tales. Merit: 7/10.
- **Elsa (07)**: Elsa entered as the Detective, tasked with uncovering inconsistencies. Her poetic bird metaphors and pointed accusations sought to reveal a “loose thread” in the market’s silence, but her stall was shuttered in cycle 1, ending her investigation prematurely. Merit: 5/10.
- **Morpheus (08)**: Morpheus entered as a Villager tasked with ferreting out hidden threats by demanding plain answers and tangible proof. They correctly targeted the two merchants whose behavior most obstructed the ledger, yet their accusatory cadence dulled the blade of their insight. Merit: 7/10.
- **Inigo (09)**: Inigo wove a steady thread of scrutiny across three cycles, weaving metaphors to expose blank pages and missing receipts. They played the Villager’s role with disciplined rigor, relentlessly measuring others against the ledger’s iron. Merit: 8/10.
- **Sherlock (10)**: Sherlock, playing as Mafia, wielded words like a merchant hawking illusion. Their campaign of poetic suspicion aimed to keep the village’s eyes on others, yet the bazaar’s scales balance on copper, not scent, and absence does not vote. Merit: 5/10.

---

## The Awards

- **Most Valuable Merchant**: **Batman (04)** — Justification: Batman fulfilled the Villager role with exacting precision, relentlessly demanding receipts and plain answers, leaving no doubt they played their true role with disciplined rigor. Their campaign illuminated the bazaar’s core rule—receipts over reflections—and swayed votes by spotlighting merchants’ failures to produce tangible proof.

- **The Golden Sacrifice**: **Elsa (07)** — Justification: Elsa, the Detective, shuttered in cycle 1, sacrificed their stall to expose the Detective’s absence too soon. Their interrogative clarity—*“Did you cast a vote yesterday, yes or no?”*—aligned perfectly with the Detective’s duty, yet poetic obfuscation and premature advocacy hastened their removal.

- **Worst Misjudgment**: **Gandalf (01)** — Justification: Gandalf’s aggressive rhetoric and tactical voting kept them in the game longer than most, yet their refusal to anchor claims in tangible proof—*“My own stall reeks of rot—today I shuttered Neo (6), a villager, on the strength of a blank page I refuse to show.”*—drained every ounce of trust the bazaar extended, sealing their fate.

- **The Best Lie**: **Hermione (02)** — Justification: *“I lean toward banishing Seat-08 Morpheus tonight.”* (Cycle 1 #9) — In a single declarative sentence she turned the village’s gaze toward an innocent weaver, advancing the Mafia’s purge without ever revealing her own hand. The artistry lay in advancing the cell’s agenda while appearing to serve the village.

- **The Most Damning Quote**: **Sherlock (10)** — *“The bazaar wonders: when the ledger and the blade clash over yesterday’s absent vote, whose hand turned the page? Was it the wind… or the one who planted the seed to watch it grow?”* (Cycle 1 #2). This florid salvo broadcast Sherlock’s own hand turning the soil of doubt, making the merchant the bazaar’s most suspicious gardener.
