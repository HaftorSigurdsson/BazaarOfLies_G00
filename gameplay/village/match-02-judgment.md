## The Main Arguments

### The Case for Silence as Alloy
The strongest argument for the Family’s survival was the weaponization of silence. Sherlock framed it as a ledger debit: *“Ripley, your iron filings scent the air like a merchant who measures twice but cuts no cloth—where is the shuttered stall your tongs should have named last night? The bazaar buys facts, not guesses.”* (Cycle 1, #6). This line forced the village to equate evasion with guilt, turning the Detective’s role against them. Gandalf echoed this, demanding plain answers while his own stall remained unexamined: *“The bazaar buys facts, not guesses, and scent alone does not shutter a stall.”* (Cycle 1, #6). The Family’s cohesion here was critical: their silence was a shield, while the village’s was a liability.

### The Case for the Loom’s Warp
The village’s strongest counter was the insistence on tangible threads. Batman crystallized this: *“Elsa, your riddle has no stitch. Sherlock asked plain: who do you fear tonight? Your answer was silence—copper turns to alloy under pressure.”* (Cycle 1, #9). This framed silence as a material failure, not an abstract flaw. Inigo later reinforced it: *“Morpheus, your iron tongue claims the Family fears two seats yet names none, while your own stall whispers in yesterday’s rust.”* (Cycle 2, #9). The village’s error was in assuming the Family’s silence was a flaw to exploit, rather than a strategy to endure.

### The Detective’s Unseen Thread
Ripley attempted to unravel the Family’s tapestry by demanding the shuttered stall: *“If the Family leaves iron behind, then where is the shuttered stall they shuttered? Name it, or step aside—this bazaar drinks facts, not thermodynamics.”* (Cycle 1, #5). The Detective’s sealed result (targeting Sherlock) was a missed opportunity; had it been revealed, the village might have pivoted sooner. Instead, Ripley’s stall was shuttered before the thread could be pulled.
