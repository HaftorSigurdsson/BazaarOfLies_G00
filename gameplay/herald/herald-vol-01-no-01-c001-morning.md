# The Herald — Morning Edition

*Vol. 1 — No. 1 — Cycle 1*

```markdown
# THE HERALD
*Est. in ink, read in haste*

**BAZAAR OPENS ITS SHUTTERS TO TEN MERCHANTS**

The shutters groaned upward at dawn, and the bazaar’s first breath of the season drew ten merchants to its stalls. By all accounts, the patience of the marketplace holds—for now.

Merchants learned at dawn that the Family numbers three, though none yet stand revealed. The Doctor and the Detective remain unaccounted for among the living, their roles still veiled by the graveyard’s silence.

The ledger begins thus: ten names, no departures, and the compact’s demand for plain speech.

---

### THE STATE OF THE BAZAAR

Ten merchants stand at their stalls this morning: **Gandalf, Hermione, Yoda, Batman, Ripley, Neo, Elsa, Morpheus, Inigo, Sherlock**.

The Family’s three remain hidden among them, their roles untested by the night’s quiet. The Doctor and the Detective, too, walk unseen—no obituary has yet claimed their offices.

The risk lies in the unknown: the first departure will reveal a role, and the bazaar will learn whether the Family’s hand has been played or spared.

The ledger favours no side yet. The day’s turning will hinge on the first vote—and the first words spoken in earnest.

---

### LETTER TO THE EDITOR
From **Elsa**:
*The shutters have opened and the bazaar breathes with silence.*
*I am Elsa, the scribe who keeps the bazaar’s record true.*
*Speak plainly, merchants, so I can set the ledger straight:*
*today’s first words will be the first lines I quote.*
*Gandalf, Hermione, Yoda, Batman, Ripley, Neo, Morpheus, Inigo, Sherlock —*
*name your claim, your fear, or your allegiance, and I will write it down.*
*The compact demands plain speech and public votes;*
*I will quote you exactly, day by day, so the bazaar sees its own truth.*
*The record begins now.*
```

## Editor's Note

The bazaar awoke to a curious quiet this morning, the usual clatter of early trade softened by an unspoken accord among the merchants—each, it seems, content to let the day’s first transactions speak for themselves. Whether this restraint stems from caution or calculation, the stalls stand orderly, the air thick with the scent of possibility rather than the usual clamor of advantage. A rare harmony, if only for the moment.
