# The Herald — Evening Edition

*Vol. 1 — No. 4 — Cycle 2*

```markdown
# THE HERALD
*Est. before memory, ink never dry*

**EVENING EDITION – CYCLE 002**
*The bazaar sleeps; the vote is cast*

---

### **INIGO BANISHED BY VILLAGE VOTE**
Merchants learned at dusk that the bazaar’s arithmetic holds. **Inigo**, the Tea Seller, has been banished by a vote of four. His role: **Villager**. The ledger now shows seven stalls standing; the graveyard holds three.

---

### **THE DEPARTED**
**Inigo** (Villager) — banished by village vote.
Last words: *"Farewell, my friends—I go where the winds take me. May your days be as bright as the sun over the bazaar."*

---

### **THE WORD ON THE STREET**
The day’s debate turned on a single clause, again. **Hermione** pressed the graveyard’s text as final, while **Sherlock** demanded the clause he invoked be quoted—nine times, by all accounts. Silence answered every demand. The bazaar’s patience, it seems, is no longer than its memory.

Quotes of the day:
**Gandalf:** *"Sherlock still refuses to quote the clause he demands, and his Lunch act named no merchant to watch."*
**Hermione:** *"The graveyard names Elsa and Batman as Villagers by plain text; arithmetic cannot amend that record."*
**Yoda:** *"Inigo dodged nine demands for Compact clause 5, stayed silent on a graveyard correction Hermione named, and voted against the ledger expecta—"*

---

### **THE STATE OF THE BAZAAR**
Seven merchants remain. The ledger shows three Villagers departed; no Family, Doctor, or Detective have fallen. The graveyard’s arithmetic is plain: three of the Family walk among the living, their roles unproven. The Doctor and Detective still stand—by the Compact’s reckoning, their duties may yet shape the night.

The risk lies in repetition. The bazaar has banished three Villagers; the unseen grow fewer. The ledger favours those who answer plainly.

---

### **BY THE NUMBERS**
- Merchants alive: 7
- Merchants departed: 3
- Cycle: 2

---

### **LETTER TO THE EDITOR**
From **Neo:**
```

## Editor's Note

The bazaar moves with the quiet tension of a market that has twice now seen its stalls thinned by vote rather than trade. Ten ballots cast aside suggest either haste or hesitation—neither of which bodes well for the evening’s commerce. Still, the remaining merchants ply their wares, though the air carries the scent of copper less brightly than before.
