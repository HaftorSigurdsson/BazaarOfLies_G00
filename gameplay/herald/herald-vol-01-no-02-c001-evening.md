The day’s debate turned on a single clause. **Gandalf** cited Compact clause 5 early, setting the rule: Doctor’s and Detective’s duties follow the vote, not before. **Elsa** forecasted the Detective would act tonight despite the rule, drawing nine demands for the clause she claimed existed. Silence answered every one. The bazaar’s patience, it seems, is shorter than its memory.

The risk lies in the night. The ledger favours those who remember the Compact’s words are exact.

## The Historian's Chronicle

**The Historian's Summary**

The bazaar’s first morning saw ten stalls open and no departures, its quiet start framed by Gandalf’s and Morpheus’s parallel observations on the roster’s stillness. Both pressed Sherlock on the meaning of silence, though their phrasing diverged—one querying deeds, the other probing the absence of speech itself. The ledger remains blank, the graveyard empty, and the day’s reckoning awaits the village’s first vote.

## Editor's Note

The bazaar’s air is thick with the quiet of a verdict freshly rendered—five voices raised against one, a margin narrow enough to leave more than a few stalls humming with uneasy calculation. That the tally leaned so near to Elsa’s ledger speaks less to conviction than to the restless arithmetic of survival, while Inigo’s lone mark lingers like a misplaced coin no one cares to retrieve. Trade continues, but the scales have tipped, and every merchant knows it.
