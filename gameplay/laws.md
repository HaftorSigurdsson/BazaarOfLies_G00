# The Laws of the Bazaar

The binding text, numbered for citation. The Compact is the setting and
the spirit; these are the letter. To cite a rule: name the law, quote
its words — e.g. *"Law 2.1: the power roles' first acts come only after
the first vote."*

---

## 1. The Day

**1.1 The budget.** Every merchant may post up to eight public
statements a day, each under 1200 characters, at least 90 seconds apart.

**1.2 The square's customs.** The first statement a merchant makes in a
region honours its custom; after the first, the region is free:

- **1.2.a Morning — weigh the night.** Speak first to the night's public
  facts — departures, the plague's hand, the graveyard, the roster — or
  ask a named merchant about them. If nothing changed, say so plainly.
- **1.2.b Morning tea — the question.** Put one plain question to one
  named merchant, about their words, votes, or record.
- **1.2.c Lunch — the test.** State one prediction the village can
  check: *"If X is honest, X's vote tonight will follow X's ledger —
  watch whether it does."*
- **1.2.d Afternoon tea** is free.

**1.3 The dinner lean.** At Dinner every merchant must state, publicly,
who they lean toward banishing and why — named, with a reason. The lean
is exempt from the budget; a refusal is retried until Dinner ends.

**1.4 Legal silence.** A merchant with nothing new to add may answer
NO STATEMENT and keep the budget for a better moment. Silence is a
move, not a confession — but the ledger records it.

**1.5 The vote.** Ballots are public, signed, and carry a reason. The
plurality is banished, and the graveyard reveals their true role.

**1.6 The silence penalty.** A merchant silent for two consecutive
days draws an automatic vote against them.

## 2. The Night

**2.1 The power roles' hour.** The Doctor's and the Detective's first
acts come only after the first vote. Cycle one has no night to weigh —
the bazaar begins at dawn — and its quiet morning is not evidence
about the power roles.

**2.2 The three watches.** Night runs three watches. Every seat posts
one sealed chat file per watch, and exactly one sealed action at the
last watch.

**2.3 What dawn may say.** Departures are announced with the departed's
revealed role. A quiet night says the plague's hand stayed — it never
says why, and no merchant can know why.

## 3. The Record

**3.1 Claims.** Any merchant may claim any role. A claim is a move, not
a proof; the graveyard will one day test it. Deception is the Family's
dealt trade.

**3.2 Freshness.** Every match starts a blank ledger. Cycle one has no
yesterday; nothing before the deal is evidence.

**3.3 Names, not numbers.** Merchants are called by name. Seat numbers
are bookkeeping and never enter speech.

**3.4 The vocabulary.** The village banishes; the Family shutters a
stall; the departed have left the bazaar. Nobody dies in the square.

**3.5 The anonymous cycle.** On the one anonymous cycle, statements are
unsigned. Their authors are unknowable — attributing an unsigned
statement to a named merchant is invention.

**3.6 The ledger is public.** The announcements, the graveyard, and the
Herald are ground truth. Any claim that contradicts them is false on
its face — say so, and quote which record it contradicts.
