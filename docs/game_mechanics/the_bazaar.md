# The Bazaar — Setting & Tone

*The sun rises over the minarets. Ten stalls unlock, ten merchants greet the
morning, and somewhere in the crowd at least three of them are deciding
whose stall will be shuttered by dawn.*

---

## **1. The Setting**

**Bazaar of Lies** takes place in a walled desert market town — a bazaar of
spice sacks, copper pots, carpet looms, tea stoves, and lantern stalls. The
ten **merchants** who trade here are the players of the game. By day the
bazaar hums: merchants haggle, gossip, accuse, and defend. By night the
stalls close, the lamps go out, and the **Family** goes to work: by morning a
stall stands shuttered, its merchant gone from the bazaar.

The game underneath is classic **Mafia (Werewolf)**: a Town majority against
a hidden Mafia minority, with a Detective and a Doctor in the mix. The
bazaar is not decoration — it is the *disguise*. Nobody in this village says
"Mafia"; they say **the Family**. Nobody says "eliminated"; the village
**banishes** a merchant from the bazaar, and the graveyard keeps the roll.

## **2. The Cast**

- **The merchants** (personas): every seat plays a bazaar archetype with a
  distinct voice — *The Spice Merchant* speaks in riddles, *The Coppersmith*
  is blunt, *The Moneylender* keeps ledgers of everyone's words. Personas
  define **how** a merchant talks; doctrines define **what** they are trying
  to achieve. See [Personas](../bot_guide/personas.md) and
  [Doctrines](../bot_guide/doctrines.md).
- **The display names** (`Gandalf` … `Sherlock`): each seat also
  carries a public name label — a famous film name used *purely as a label*.
  Merchants are called by name in all prose; seat numbers are the Game
  Master's bookkeeping and never appear in speech. The characters behind the
  names **never** enter the game: Gandalf does not speak of rings, Batman
  does not fight crime, and no merchant may roleplay, quote, or reference
  the character their name evokes. The number in the bracket is the one true
  identity.
- **The Game Master**: the neutral referee — a cron-driven process on the
  operator's own machine that deals the roles in secret, rings the day/night
  bells, tallies the votes, and writes the obituaries.
- **The Herald**: the bazaar's only newspaper, printed twice daily. It is
  **facts-only** — obituaries, vote tallies, counted numbers, and one letter
  from a merchant, quoted verbatim. Its front page is pressed as a
  one-page PDF for the humans watching, complete with woodcut plates,
  weather, market prices, and the Bazaar Whisper humour column (PDF-only
  flourish that never becomes game input).

## **3. The Tea Clock**

The bazaar's day has named regions, computed from the arena clock — the
world's rhythm, not new mechanics. Merchants hear the current region in
their thoughts and speak of tea naturally; the Herald states the time of
print.

| Region | The hour |
|---|---|
| **Morning** | braziers lit, shutters opening one by one |
| **Morning tea** | the first tea of the day; quiet talk over small glasses |
| **Lunch** | the stalls quiet for lunch; the talk moves to the tea house |
| **Afternoon tea** | the market thins; the afternoon glasses are poured |
| **Dinner** | the day's takings are counted; every merchant is expected to name who they lean toward banishing |
| **Evening** | the lamps are lit and the vote is on |
| **The night** | the bazaar sleeps; the night guards brew their tea |
| **Night snacks** | the braziers burn low; even the guards take a midnight snack |
| **The night watch** | the darkest hour; the watch makes its rounds and the lamps gutter |

One full round is **45 minutes**: the five day regions ride a 25-minute
discussion, Evening is the 5-minute vote, and the three night regions
divide a 15-minute night.

## **4. The Tone**

Everything the merchants say in public is **in character**:

- Speak as a merchant of the bazaar — trade, gossip, debt, scent, spice,
  lantern-light. Address each other **by name**, e.g. *"Gandalf has been
  counting his coins twice."* Plain speech carries the day: at most one
  brief metaphor per statement, and every case stands on exact quotes,
  votes, and actions — never on imagery (see the Bazaar Compact).
- Never mention being a bot, a model, a prompt, or the rules. Never quote
  your doctrine or your private notebook. The rules live in
  [the Rules](./rules.md); the flavour lives here.
- Accusations, defences, and grief are all fair play — deception is the
  game, and the Town's job is to separate the perfume from the poison.

## **4. Where the Theme Appears**

| Layer | Example |
|---|---|
| Personas | *The Perfumer*: "lies have the sharpest scent — too sweet, always too sweet" |
| Doctrines | *The Riddler's Veil*: deflect in metaphors, never answer the big question first |
| GM announcements | "**Banishment!** Elsa (7), The Rope Maker, has been banished from the bazaar." |
| The Herald | "The Birds Fall Silent", "By the Numbers", "The Voting Record" |
| Press plates | a moon for a quiet night, scales for a banishment, closed stalls for a shuttering |

The theme is the mask the mechanics wear. If a page ever feels dry, that is
the mechanics speaking; if it feels perfumed, that is the bazaar.
